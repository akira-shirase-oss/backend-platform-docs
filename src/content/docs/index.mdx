The Backend Platform is a self-hosted control plane in the Appwrite/Supabase direction:
one developer experience over deployment, identity, data, files, jobs and diagnostics,
assembled from proven components rather than rewritten infrastructure. Its purpose is to
make small applications easier to *operate* — provisioning is a reconciled process,
running applications outlive the control plane, and upgrade, restore and export are
rehearsed drills, not runbook folklore.

## Honest status

The project is **parked**: the maintainer has not yet decided to build the control plane
(the "unpark" question is open). What exists in the repository today was built to answer
the "is a platform worth it" question with evidence rather than enthusiasm:

- **B0 — the spine (built and green).** The control API, the reconciler worker and its
  append-only operation log, the fault-injecting fake provider, and the
  partial-provisioning drill. The milestone's own evidence memo recommended *staying
  parked* and meeting the need differently — the recommendation was accepted.
- **B1-T — the starter template (in progress).** A copy-and-own Rust service skeleton:
  a bounded HTTP layer, a hardened PostgreSQL split, an accounts kit, the health
  contract, releases, restore drills and the CI + evidence kit. A product copies it once
  and owns it. Everything except its final acceptance run on a droplet is built and proven.

The control-plane milestones B1–B7 are **planned, not built** — described here honestly
as plans in [Control plane](/control-plane/). Nothing in these docs presents the parked
platform as runnable product.

## What it is not

Not a shared login or account service for the sibling products (Colophon, Service
Monitor, Durable Runner). Those products keep their own accounts and databases and are
not tenants of this platform; the accounts-work they share is why the *template* exists —
code each copies, not a service they depend on.
